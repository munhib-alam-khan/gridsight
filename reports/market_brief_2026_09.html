September 2026 averaged Rs 32.4/kWh, up 1% on the month before and up 98% on September 2025.

227 of its 720 hours (32%) were at or above Rs 50, with a high of Rs 93.9 at 21:00 on 08 Sep.

The day split in two: midday hours (10:00-16:00) averaged Rs 15.8 while the night (00:00-06:00) averaged Rs 40.3. Solar output pushes cheap plants onto the margin at midday; after sunset the system falls back on expensive thermal plants.

For bulk consumers weighing open access

At September's prices, a 5 MW B-3 factory running around the clock would have paid Rs 48.7/kWh buying at the hourly market price through the wheeling auction, against Rs 30.6/kWh on its DISCO tariff: Rs 18.1/kWh more. Energy alone, grossed up for network losses, came to Rs 36.3/kWh.

The highest fixed contract price at which open access still breaks even for this factory: Rs 18.9/kWh at the generator through the auction, Rs 7.3/kWh outside it (where the Rs 12.94 stranded-cost charge applies).
Grid charges per NEPRA's uniform Use of System Charges decision (7 Sep 2026); losses, capacity and ancillary costs as in ISMO's CTBCM model; regulated tariff per NEPRA decision TRF-100 of 11 Feb 2026. Excludes sales tax and duty, which apply to both routes.
